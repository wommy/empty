ci: normalize real-ZFS weights per part by its fork count

A test measured in the loaded endpoint part read 2-4x higher than the
same test in the light one, so the weights carried each part's
contention, and the plan swung tests between parts at inflated weights.

ci/real-zfs-weights.sh now scales each part's rows (one run, one part)
to sum to that part's fork count, from the "forks N" line of the
guest's load report (in the job log, or load.txt beside an artifact's
TSV). Without one it falls back to the root cgroup's CPU-seconds, and
with neither for every part it keeps raw seconds and says so. It then
averages runs and rescales to the raw seconds' total. A parallel test,
or one with parallel subtests, now weighs cost / nproc from both a TSV
and an older log: the TSV gave the crash test its wall instead.

Regenerated from runs 40550837228, 40551637783, 40551837712 and
40552465632. Predicted part 0 share of the forks: 44.8% before, 50.1%
after. The probation note on part 0's 1.3-1.5x inflation is superseded
and dropped. The +0.10 for TestDestroyNeverHitsAReplacedDataset is
re-derived on the new scale in its own note, and the sim-only rows stay
0.00. ci/weightstest checks the script on two-part fixtures whose
weights are known.
